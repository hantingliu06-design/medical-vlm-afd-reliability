# Chapter 1: Introduction

Medical image interpretation is question driven. The question determines
which structure, finding, or relation matters, and visual question
answering (VQA) turns this interaction into a computational task by
pairing an image with a natural-language question and generating an
answer [@agrawal2016vqa; @he2020pathvqa]. This interface may support
focused information access in clinical education, image review,
diagnosis, and treatment planning, but its value depends on both the
answer and the ability to recognise when that answer is unreliable.

Clinical Motivation
===================

Question-driven systems have been studied in surgical, pathology, and
radiology images. Surgical-VQA showed that transformer models could
answer questions about instruments, anatomy, actions, and locations in
operative scenes [@seenivasan2022surgicalvqa], while PathVQA and VQA-RAD
extended medical VQA to pathology and radiology questions with both
closed and open answers [@he2020pathvqa; @lau2018vqarad].
Foundation-model research now considers systems that combine images,
language, and clinical context across several tasks
[@moor2023foundation]. This creates a possible route from simple image
description towards diagnostic and treatment decision support, but it
also raises the cost of unsupported answers because a plausible phrase
may influence clinical interpretation.

The Safety Problem
==================

VLMs can produce fluent and confident answers that are not supported by
the image. Reported failure types include invented objects, incorrect
attributes, unsupported relations, and responses driven by language
priors [@liu2024hallucinationsurvey]. Average benchmark performance
cannot show whether these errors occur silently in difficult cases, and
a model that answers many cases well may still give weak warnings when
it fails. Responsible healthcare AI therefore requires evaluation of
plausible failure modes, human oversight, and the consequences of
accepting an incorrect output [@wiens2019donoharm]. The practical
question is not only whether a model can answer, but whether unreliable
answers can be identified before they are used.

From Accuracy to Reliability
============================

Medical VQA commonly measures answer quality with BLEU, ROUGE-L, and
METEOR, which describe different forms of overlap between a generated
answer and a reference
[@papineni2002bleu; @lin2004rouge; @banerjee2005meteor]. These metrics
support reproducible comparison but do not fully represent medical
correctness: a valid synonym can receive limited credit, while an
incorrect phrase can retain words from the reference. They also do not
show whether the model recognises a weak answer. Uncertainty and
automatic failure detection address this second property by assigning
case-level scores, but confidence does not automatically track
correctness and higher task performance does not guarantee better
calibration [@guo2017calibration; @jiang2021whenknow]. Answer quality
and failure awareness must therefore be measured separately.

Why the Relationship May Diverge
================================

Black-box methods can sample several answers and treat disagreement as
evidence of uncertainty. Semantic methods improve on exact matching by
grouping paraphrases, but repeated answers can still be consistently
wrong or unrelated to the question
[@farquhar2024semanticentropy; @nguyen2025pairwisesemantic].
Question-aligned uncertainty in surgical VQA was motivated by this
difference between answer consistency and question-conditioned validity
[@carlini2026qasnne]. Reliability may also change with image modality,
question style, answer length, and clinical context because uncertainty
estimates often deteriorate or change under dataset shift
[@ovadia2019uncertainty]. A model or detector ranking observed in one
medical task cannot therefore be assumed to hold in another.

Problem Framing
===============

Two research lines now meet at this problem. One improves the answers
generated by general and medically adapted VLMs, while the other
develops uncertainty, failure-detection, and abstention methods
[@li2023llavamed; @sellergren2025medgemma; @geifman2017selective]. They
are often evaluated separately, leaving unclear whether stronger answer
generation is accompanied by stronger failure awareness. This
dissertation addresses that gap by comparing Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct,
LLaVA-1.5-7B, and MedGemma-4B-it on PathVQA, VQA-RAD, and
ProstateMM-CHIMERA. Answer utility and operational failure detection are
reported as separate dimensions so that agreement or divergence between
their model rankings can be observed rather than assumed.
