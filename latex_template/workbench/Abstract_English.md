# Abstract

Vision-language models (VLMs) with strong answer-generation performance are increasingly being explored for clinical diagnosis and treatment decision support, but safe use also requires unreliable answers to be recognised. Current evaluations mainly reward answer accuracy, and it remains unclear whether reliability and failure awareness improve with accuracy or diverge from it. We evaluate Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, LLaVA-1.5-7B, and MedGemma-4B-it in a zero-shot setting on the complete PathVQA test split, with VQA-RAD and ProstateMM-CHIMERA as external validation, using BLEU-1, BLEU-2, ROUGE-L, and METEOR for answer quality and AUROC, AUPRC, and selective coverage analysis for failure detection. On PathVQA, MedGemma-4B-it achieves the highest ROUGE-L and METEOR scores but the lowest best-detector AUROC of 68.63%, whereas Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct leads BLEU-1 and BLEU-2 and achieves the highest AUROC of 81.20%; the answer-quality–failure-detection divergence is clearer on VQA-RAD, but it is not reproduced on the smaller ProstateMM-CHIMERA test set. These findings show that better answer quality does not necessarily imply better failure awareness and that healthcare VLMs should be evaluated on both utility and operational reliability before clinical use.
